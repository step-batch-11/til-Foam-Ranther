# four stage of competence :

 1. Unconscious Incompetence – The individual does not understand or know how to do
something and is unaware of the deficit. They may deny the usefulness of the
skill. This stage is characterized by blissful ignorance, where one doesn’t know
what they don’t know.

2. Conscious Incompetence – The individual recognizes their
lack of skill and understands the value of learning it. Mistakes are common and
integral to growth. This stage often brings frustration but is crucial for
motivation to improve.

3. Conscious Competence – The individual can perform the
skill, but it requires full concentration and effort. The task is broken down
into steps, and performance depends on deliberate practice and focus.

4. Unconscious Competence – The skill has become second nature. It can be performed
effortlessly, often while multitasking, and may be taught to others. Mastery is
achieved, and the behavior feels intuitive.
